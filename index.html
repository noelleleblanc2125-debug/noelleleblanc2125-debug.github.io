A collection of screenshots, thoughts, and reflections on figuring out what adulthood looks like today.

About the Project click to open

Young adulthood is often framed as the time when we're supposed to finally “figure it out”—who we are, what we want, and what our adult lives are going to look like.

But online, a different story emerges.

Across social media, people in their twenties share career changes, shifting relationships, financial uncertainty, questions about marriage and family, changing priorities, and the strange realization that adulthood doesn't always feel the way we expected it to.

This project collects those moments to explore young adulthood not as a finished identity, but as an ongoing process of becoming. Through these posts, I am interested in how social media gives young adults a space to express, navigate, and collectively make sense of uncertainty.

What I'm Capturing click to open

For this collection, I am looking for publicly available media that reflects a disrupted expectation, an unfamiliar experience, or a moment of reflection about what it means to establish an adult identity in your twenties.

That might look like:

  • uncertainty about a career or life direction
  • changing relationships with family, friends, or romantic partners
  • questions about marriage or having children
  • navigating financial or residential independence
  • comparing the adulthood we imagined with the adulthood we're actually experiencing
  • changing values, priorities, or understandings of ourselves
  • feeling “behind,” uncertain, or simply still figuring things out

Not every post will challenge traditional ideas about adulthood. Some may reinforce familiar expectations, milestones, or timelines. Those moments belong here, too. They provide a point of comparison and help show the different ideas about adulthood that young people encounter online.

How I'm Collecting click to open

Most of this collection comes from Instagram and Facebook—particularly posts that appear naturally while scrolling through my feeds and discovery pages.

Rather than searching specifically for content that proves my argument, I regularly browse these spaces and capture relevant posts as I encounter them. This allows the collection to reflect the kinds of messages about adulthood that someone might come across during ordinary social media use.

I am also keeping an eye out for relevant media beyond social platforms. While these examples may appear less frequently, they can offer another perspective on the expectations, uncertainties, and contradictions surrounding life in your twenties.
