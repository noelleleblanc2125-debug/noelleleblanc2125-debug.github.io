Relationships & Family

☆

Posts about changing relationships with family, friends, and romantic partners, as well as the expectations surrounding love, marriage, and building a family in young adulthood.

♡

The speaker is talking about his longtime partner. This contrasts with the expectation I had as a child that love and marriage were always something positive. It changes my view on relationships now that I am older. It also suggests that this outcome is not always intentional.

Earlier in this post and series of images, the speaker was talking about how their focus in university was purely academics. But as the post goes on, they come to realize that university was about the friends they made. And I think that truly is a staple of the phase of young adulthood. I think a common experience I hear a lot of people talk about are the friendships they made in college and how those have carried through their life. This post also made me think that these are the people who are growing into the people you are becoming and they are doing it alongside you.

This screenshot paired with the others really highlighted a relationship I had previously overlooked before: the one with yourself. This has made me think about the relationship with yourself and how that is always changing, but also being able to acknowledge that maybe we have become someone new. That we have changed and grown so much that we have become a different person.

A reminder that even when you feel lost, your friends are there to remind you of who you are.

This screenshot is a part of a series of posts that talk about a couple's approach to anniversary gifts. This couple has stopped getting each other big gifts and getting their partner's things that will make the other's life easier, better and I that this screenshot contrasts what I imagined young adult relationships were like when I was younger. Before I thought it was so passionate, so outgoing, and so whimsy. But I love the quiet, the calm, and the mundane and how the person you plan to spend the rest of your life notices the little things makes us feel seen and loved. It's not the big extravagant gifts.

"I've realized love rarley announces itself when it enters the room." I used to think that showing one's love was through the actions that were named, but as I grow I have started to see showing my love like this screenshots. It's not the things I've bought. It's not me spending my money on a nice dinner date. It's making lunch when I can tell they're overwhelmed. It's the things that are not and do not need to be named.
